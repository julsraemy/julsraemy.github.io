## [In a nutshell]

I'm [Julien Antoine Raemy][name] (he/him), originally from Fribourg, Switzerland. Trained as a bookseller, I went on to study Information Science at the [Haute école de gestion de Genève][heg] (HES-SO), then earned a PhD in Digital Humanities at the University of Basel.

My work focuses on improving the digital preservation, dissemination and interoperability of cultural heritage and its metadata through open standards built on the [Linked Open Usable Data (LOUD)][phd] design principles. I'm an active member of the [International Image Interoperability Framework (IIIF)][iiif], [Linked Art][linkedart] and [ARK Alliance][ark] communities.

## [How I got here]

<div class="about-timeline">
<div class="about-timeline__item" markdown="1">
<p class="about-timeline__years">2008–2013</p>

Books came first. I completed my Certificate of Vocational Education and Training as a bookseller in 2008, followed by a six-month internship in the [ELT Marketing Department of Oxford University Press][elt] in the UK. I then spent some time teaching English in Poland and working in Australia, trying to work out what to do next.
</div>

<div class="about-timeline__item" markdown="1">
<p class="about-timeline__years">2013–2020</p>

The answer turned out to be information science. Between 2013 and 2017, I completed a Bachelor in Information Science at [HEG-GE][heg] while working as a Photo Archivist for the Montreux Jazz Digital Project at [EPFL's MetaMedia Center][metamedia], which was later renamed _Cultural Heritage & Innovation Center_. I stayed on at HEG-GE as a Research and Teaching Assistant, helping to coordinate the bilingual LIS curriculum, and completed my [Master's degree in Information Science][master-is] along the way.
</div>

<div class="about-timeline__item" markdown="1">
<p class="about-timeline__years">2020–2024</p>

In 2020, I briefly worked as a Knowledge Graph Engineer and IIIF Systems Architect for the [Swiss Art Research Infrastructure][sari] before starting a funded PhD at the University of Basel. From 2021 to 2024, I was part of the [Digital Humanities Lab][dhlab] on the [Participatory Knowledge Practices in Analogue and Digital Image Archives][pia] (PIA) research project, while also working as an Interoperability Specialist at [DaSCH][dasch]. In November 2024, I successfully defended my thesis, [Linked Open Usable Data for Cultural Heritage: Perspectives on Community Practices and Semantic Interoperability][phd].
</div>

<div class="about-timeline__item" markdown="1">
<p class="about-timeline__years">2024–2025</p>

From April 2024 to December 2025, I was a Data Scientist and Project Manager at the [Swiss Federal Archives][bar], where I coordinated the development of LINDASnext, a platform enabling public administrations to publish and visualise their data as knowledge graphs.
</div>
</div>
